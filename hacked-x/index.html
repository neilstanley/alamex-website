The first hour, in order

  1. Open your profile logged out. Screenshot it and several scam posts, with dates.
  2. Keep X's "email changed" notice. Start a log, with times in UTC.
  3. Tap "Secure your account" in that notice and file the compromised-account form. Once.
  4. Try the password reset once and screenshot what it offers. If the reset goes to the attacker's address, stop.
  5. Reply to X's automatic questions within minutes: username, every email, last access date, phone.
  6. Search every mailbox for x.com and twitter.com. Save it all. Old receipts and notices are your proof.
  7. Change your email password and switch on two-step verification there.
  8. Warn people where the attacker can't reach: LinkedIn, groups, email.
  9. Ignore anyone who offers to help in DMs or replies. They are scammers too.
  10. Do not create a new account. Do not delete anything yet.
Then expect a no. X's access team rejected my proof twice, with the same email word for word. Closed did not mean closed. What I did next was send five things on the same morning, each citing the others. Two days later, X moved.
